### [1] A transformer \citep{vaswani2017} composes its layers through residual connections \citep{he2016resnet}: every attention head and MLP reads the same running sum and writes back into it.

- vaswani2017: SUPPORTED — the Transformer wraps each attention and feed-forward sub-layer in a residual connection. Sec. 3.1 (Encoder and Decoder Stacks): "We employ a residual connection [11] around each of the two sub-layers, followed by layer normalization [1]. That is, the output of each sub-layer is LayerNorm(x+Sublayer(x)), where Sublayer(x) is the function implemented by the sub-layer itself." and (Decoder) "Similar to the encoder, we employ residual connections around each of the sub-layers, followed by layer normalization." (also in earlier notes). Minor note: the original is post-LN, so the sum is re-normalized between sub-layers; the "same running sum" picture is exact for the pre-norm/OLMo-2-style blocks used in the paper. No change needed.
- he2016resnet: SUPPORTED — introduces identity-shortcut residual learning. Abstract: "We present a residual learning framework to ease the training of networks that are substantially deeper than those used previously. We explicitly reformulate the layers as learning residual functions with reference to the layer inputs, instead of learning unreferenced functions." Sec. 1: "In our case, the shortcut connections simply perform identity mapping, and their outputs are added to the outputs of the stacked layers (Fig. 2)." (also in earlier notes)

### [2] Mechanistic analyses describe this residual stream as the communication channel between components \citep{elhage2021}, and its capacity is shared by every layer, so the cross-layer connectivity of a transformer is both fixed and crowded.

- elhage2021: SUPPORTED — both the "communication channel" framing and the shared/in-demand capacity are stated. Section "Virtual Weights and the Residual Stream as a Communication Channel": "The residual stream is simply the sum of the output of all the previous layers and the original embedding. We generally think of the residual stream as a communication channel, since it doesn't do any processing itself and all layers communicate through it." Subsection "Subspaces and Residual Stream Bandwidth": "It seems like we should expect residual stream bandwidth to be in very high demand! There are generally far more "computational dimensions" (such as neurons and attention head result dimensions) than the residual stream has dimensions to move information." Appendix, "Bottleneck Activations": "it's also the only pathway by which arbitrary early MLP layers can communicate with arbitrary late MLP layers, so the stream may simultaneously be conveying different pieces of information between many different pairs of MLP layers, a much more extreme bottleneck than just 4x!" (also in earlier notes). "Crowded" paraphrases "very high demand"; "fixed" is the draft's own observation and is not attributed to the source. No change needed.

### [3] A line of work relaxes this by letting a layer read earlier layers' outputs directly: dense connections \citep{huang2017densenet}, depth-weighted averaging with learned static weights \citep{pagliardini2024denseformer}, and, most recently, hyper-connections \citep{zhu2024hyperconnections,mhc2026}, which make the mixing weights input-dependent, and multiway dynamic dense connections \citep{muddformer2025}, which also make them separate for the query, key, value and residual inputs of a block.

- huang2017densenet: SUPPORTED — every layer reads all earlier layers' outputs. Abstract: "introduce the Dense Convolutional Network (DenseNet), which connects each layer to every other layer in a feed-forward fashion." and "For each layer, the feature-maps of all preceding layers are used as inputs, and its own feature-maps are used as inputs into all subsequent layers." (also in earlier notes; DenseNet concatenates rather than averages, which the sentence does not contradict.)
- pagliardini2024denseformer: SUPPORTED — a learned, static weighted average over all earlier block outputs. Abstract: "Our approach relies on an additional averaging step after each transformer block, which computes a weighted average of current and past representations -- we refer to this operation as Depth-Weighted-Average (DWA)." Sec. 2: "using static (as opposed to dynamic) attention weights"; Sec. 3: "The elements of the alpha matrix are the only additional parameters of our method." (also in earlier notes)
- zhu2024hyperconnections: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — the input-dependence is right for the dynamic variant, but hyper-connections do not let a layer "read earlier layers' outputs directly"; they widen the residual into n parallel streams and mix those. Sec. 1: "To further enhance flexibility, we introduce dynamic hyper-connections (DHC), enabling the network to adjust connection weights according to the input." Sec. 2.2: "The entries of HC can dynamically depend on the input H". But Sec. 2.1: "Initially, h^0 in R^d is replicated n times to form the initial hyper hidden matrix H^0 ... Here, n is the expansion rate. For the k-th layer, the input consists of the hyper hidden matrix from the previous layer H^{k-1}"; Fig. 2 caption: the weights "are learnable scalars or scalars predicted by the network, depending on the specific HC version" (Sec. 2.1 is "Static Hyper-Connections", so input-dependence is optional, not definitional). The dense-connection reading is only an unfolded reinterpretation in Sec. 4.5: "we convert hyper-connections to dense connections cross layers. Consider the input hidden vectors h_0^k in k-th layer, it can be unfolded as a weighted summation over previous layer outputs". What the source says: HC replaces the single residual stream by n streams with learnable (static) or input-predicted (dynamic) depth- and width-connection weights; a layer reads a mixture of the n current streams, not earlier layers' outputs. Corrected wording below.
- mhc2026: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — mHC does compute input-dependent coefficients, but only because it inherits HC's dynamic mappings; its contribution is the manifold constraint, and it shares the "widened residual stream, not direct reads" caveat. Sec. 3 (describing HC): "learnable mappings are composed of two parts of coefficients: the input-dependent one and the global one, referred to as dynamic mappings and static mappings, respectively." Sec. 4.2: "Given the input hidden matrix x_l in R^{n x C} at the l-th layer, we first flatten it into a vector ... Then, we follow the original HC formulation to get the dynamic mappings and the static mappings". Abstract: HC works by "expanding the residual stream width and diversifying connectivity patterns", and mHC "projects the residual connection space of HC onto a specific manifold to restore the identity mapping property". Attributing "make the mixing weights input-dependent" to mHC misstates what it adds. Corrected wording below.
- muddformer2025: SUPPORTED — dynamic per-position weights, separate for the four input streams. Abstract: "MUDD generates connection weights dynamically depending on hidden states at each sequence position and for each decoupled input stream (the query, key, value or residual) of a Transformer block." Sec. 2.3: "we first turn a normal Transformer block B(X) into a multi-input one B'(X^Q, X^K, X^V, X^R) by decoupling its input into four streams for query, key, value and residual, respectively (Eq. (7), Figure 2 (e) bottom), and then instantiate four DA modules, each specializing in one stream's dense connectivity" (also in earlier notes). Note that MUDDFormer, not HC/mHC, is the one that reads earlier layers' outputs directly with input-dependent weights (Sec. 2: "the input to layer i+1 is an aggregation ... of outputs of all i+1 preceding layers").

  Proposed wording for [3]: "A line of work relaxes this. Dense connections \citep{huang2017densenet} and depth-weighted averaging with learned static weights \citep{pagliardini2024denseformer} let a layer read earlier layers' outputs directly; hyper-connections \citep{zhu2024hyperconnections} instead widen the residual stream into several streams whose mixing weights are learned and, in the dynamic variant, input-dependent, and mHC \citep{mhc2026} constrains those mixing matrices so the scheme trains stably at scale; most recently, multiway dynamic dense connections \citep{muddformer2025} compute input-dependent, per-position weights over all earlier layers' outputs, separately for the query, key, value and residual inputs of a block."

### [13] Patching \citep{vig2020causal,meng2022rome,zhang2024patching} says the same: swapping the predictor's per-token output between windows, consistently across layers or independently per layer, changes the loss by at most 0.007 nats on 75M, 150M and 300M checkpoints, while the same swap on the local channel costs 0.56 to 1.29 nats, growing with size, with a negative coherence gap (independent donors partially average out, as expected of independent per-layer routers).

(2) We refer to T as the transform gate and C as the carry gate, since they express how much of the output is produced by transforming the input and carrying it, respectively." (also in earlier notes)
- he2016resnet: SUPPORTED — identity skips. Sec. 1: "In our case, the shortcut connections simply perform identity mapping, and their outputs are added to the outputs of the stacked layers (Fig. 2). Identity shortcut connections add neither extra parameter nor computational complexity." (also in earlier notes)
- huang2017densenet: SUPPORTED — Abstract: "connects each layer to every other layer in a feed-forward fashion. Whereas traditional convolutional networks with L layers have L connections - one between each layer and its subsequent layer - our network has L(L+1)/2 direct connections." (also in earlier notes)

### [17] In NLP, transparent attention \citep{bapna2018transparent} and ELMo's scalar mixture \citep{peters2018elmo} learn static weights over layer outputs, as does DenseFormer's depth-weighted averaging for decoder-only transformers \citep{pagliardini2024denseformer} and LAuReL \citep{menghani2024laurel}.

- bapna2018transparent: SUPPORTED — learned (static) softmax weights over all encoder layer outputs. Sec. 2.4: "To improve gradient flow we let the decoder attend weighted combinations of all encoder layer outputs, instead of just the top encoder layer." and "In transparent attention we evaluate M weighted combinations of the encoder outputs, one corresponding to each attention module. We define a (N+1) x M weight vector W, which is learned during training." (also in earlier notes). The weights are parameters, not functions of the input.
- peters2018elmo: SUPPORTED — a task-specific learned scalar softmax mixture over biLM layers. Sec. 3.2: "More generally, we compute a task specific weighting of all biLM layers: ELMo_k^task = E(R_k; Theta^task) = gamma^task sum_{j=0}^{L} s_j^task h_{k,j}^LM. (1) In (1), s^task are softmax-normalized weights and the scalar parameter gamma^task allows the task model to scale the entire ELMo vector." (also in earlier notes)
- pagliardini2024denseformer: SUPPORTED — static learned DWA weights, in autoregressive LMs. Sec. 3: "The only change to the original architecture is the addition of a Depth Weighted Average module (DWA) after each transformer block. ... The elements of the alpha matrix are the only additional parameters of our method."; Sec. 2: "using static (as opposed to dynamic) attention weights". On "decoder-only": the paper never uses that term; it studies "the standard Transformer architecture" on "language modeling tasks" (OpenWebText2, PG-19) and notes that "at inference, the outputs are stored in the KV cache to facilitate auto-regressive decoding" (Sec. 3.1), i.e. decoder-only LMs. Accurate; optionally write "for autoregressive language models".
- menghani2024laurel: SUPPORTED — learned static scalars/linear maps over earlier outputs, in the LAuReL-PA variant. Sec. 2: "x_{i+1} = alpha . f(x_i) + g(x_i, x_{i-1}, ..., x_0). (2) Here, alpha is a learned scalar parameter, and g(.) is a learned linear function with x_i, x_{i-1}, ..., x_0 as inputs, where x_j is the output of the j-th residual connection." Sec. 2.3 (LAuReL-PA): "we set g(x_i, ..., x_0) = x_i + sum_{j=0}^{k-1} gamma_{i,j} . h_i(x_{i-j}), where gamma_{i,0}, ..., gamma_{i,k-1} are learned scalar parameters and h_i is another linear function." (also in earlier notes). Caveat: only the PA variant mixes earlier layers (k previous activations through a low-rank or identity map h_i); LAuReL-RW/LR only reweight or transform the current residual. "LAuReL's previous-activations variant" would be exact.

### [18] Hyper-connections \citep{zhu2024hyperconnections} widen the residual stream into several streams with learnable, optionally input-dependent, depth and width connections; mHC \citep{mhc2026} shows that the product of unconstrained mixing matrices across depth lets forward and backward signals explode or vanish, and projects the residual-mixing matrix onto the doubly stochastic manifold with Sinkhorn-Knopp to stabilize training at scale; in both, the coefficients of a layer are a function of that layer's incoming hidden state.

- zhu2024hyperconnections: SUPPORTED — this is an accurate description of HC. Sec. 2.1: "Initially, h^0 in R^d is replicated n times to form the initial hyper hidden matrix H^0 ... Here, n is the expansion rate." Fig. 2 caption: the weights "are learnable scalars or scalars predicted by the network, depending on the specific HC version. ... They can be decoupled into depth-connections and width-connections. (c) Depth-connections perform a weighted sum between the layer output and the hidden vector h_1. (d) Width-connections allow information exchange between the hidden vectors h_1 and h_2." Sec. 2.2: "The entries of HC can dynamically depend on the input H" with Eqs. (10)-(13): "H-bar = norm(H) ... B(H) = s_beta ∘ tanh(H-bar W_beta)^T + B ... A_m(H) = s_alpha ∘ tanh(H-bar W_m) + A_m ... A_r(H) = s_alpha ∘ tanh(H-bar W_r) + A_r", i.e. the layer's coefficients are a function of the hyper hidden matrix H entering that layer, plus static matrices (also in earlier notes). The final clause holds for the dynamic variant (the sentence already says "optionally input-dependent"); "in the dynamic variants of both" would be exact.
- mhc2026: SUPPORTED — all three attributed points are stated. Sec. 3.1: "Since the learnable mapping H^res_l is unconstrained, this composite mapping inevitably deviates from the identity mapping. Consequently, the signal magnitude is prone to explosion or vanishing during both the forward pass and backpropagation." Sec. 1: "mHC utilizes the Sinkhorn-Knopp algorithm (Sinkhorn and Knopp, 1967) to entropically project H^res_l onto the Birkhoff polytope. This operation effectively constrains the residual connection matrices within the manifold that is constituted by doubly stochastic matrices." Sec. 5.2 (27B models): "mHC effectively mitigates the training instability observed in HC, achieving a final loss reduction of 0.021 compared to the baseline." Sec. 4.2 (coefficients): "Given the input hidden matrix x_l in R^{n x C} at the l-th layer, we first flatten it into a vector x-vec_l = vec(x_l) in R^{1 x nC} to preserve full context information. Then, we follow the original HC formulation to get the dynamic mappings and the static mappings" (also in earlier notes). "Projects the residual-mixing matrix" is precisely right: only H^res is Sinkhorn-projected; H^pre and H^post get a sigmoid non-negativity constraint (Sec. 4.1: "Additionally, we impose non-negativity constraints on the input mappings H^pre_l and output mappings H^post_l.").

### [19] MUDDFormer \citep{muddformer2025} generates per-position dense connection weights for the query, key, value and residual streams from the hidden state at each layer and reports matching transformers trained with 1.8 to 2.4$\times$ the compute.

- muddformer2025: SUPPORTED — Abstract: "MUDD generates connection weights dynamically depending on hidden states at each sequence position and for each decoupled input stream (the query, key, value or residual) of a Transformer block." and "achieving the performance of Transformers trained with 1.8X-2.4X compute." Sec. 2.2, Eq. (6): "We instantiate A_i : R^D -> R^{i+1} with an MLP parameterized by W_1 and W_2 which computes connection weights position-wise: A_i(X_i) = GELU(RMSNorm(X_i) W_1) W_2 + a_i" (also in earlier notes). "From the hidden state at each layer" = from X_i, the output of layer i that feeds layer i+1.

### [20] \paragraph{Per-token routing.} Mixture-of-experts layers route tokens to experts with a per-layer gate on the current representation \citep{shazeer2017moe,fedus2022switch}, and Mixture-of-Depths routes tokens around blocks the same way \citep{raposo2024mod}.

- shazeer2017moe: SUPPORTED — a trainable gating network per MoE layer, applied to the layer input at every position. Abstract: "A trainable gating network determines a sparse combination of these experts to use for each example." Sec. 2.1: "Softmax Gating: A simple choice of non-sparse gating function (Jordan & Jacobs, 1994) is to multiply the input by a trainable weight matrix W_g and then apply the Softmax function. G_sigma(x) = Softmax(x . W_g)". Sec. 1.2: "The MoE is called once for each position in the text, selecting a potentially different combination of experts at each position." (first two also in earlier notes). Note: applied "convolutionally between stacked LSTM layers", not in a Transformer; the per-layer-gate-on-the-current-representation claim is still exactly what it does.
- fedus2022switch: SUPPORTED — Sec. 2.1: "Shazeer et al. (2017) proposed a natural language Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) layer which takes as an input a token representation x and then routes this to the best determined top-k experts, selected from a set {E_i(x)}_{i=1}^N of N experts. The router variable W_r produces logits h(x) = W_r . x which are normalized via a softmax distribution over the available N experts at that layer." and "we instead use a simplified strategy where we route to only a single expert. ... This k=1 routing strategy is later referred to as a Switch layer." (also in earlier notes)
- raposo2024mod: SUPPORTED — a per-block linear router on each token's current embedding decides whether the token takes the block or the residual path. Sec. 3.2: "we route tokens to one of two computational paths: (1) self-attention and MLP blocks, and (2) a residual connection." Sec. 3.4: "each token is processed by a router to produce a scalar weight, and the top-k weights are then used to choose the token identities that will route through a transformer's block, which comprises self-attention and the subsequent MLP." and "The router weight for a given token embedding is a scalar produced as a result of a linear projection, r_i^l = w_theta^T x_i^l." (also in earlier notes). "The same way" is fair at the level of the sentence (per-layer linear gate on the token's current representation); the selection rule differs (top-k over tokens in the sequence rather than softmax over experts), which the sentence does not claim.

### [21] \paragraph{Structured pruning.} We prune heads \citep{michel2019heads} and MLP channels with Taylor importance \citep{molchanov2019taylor} on a gradual schedule \citep{zhu2017prune}, the unit set of CoFi \citep{xia2022cofi}; Sheared LLaMA \citep{xia2023sheared} shows that structured pruning plus continued training is a competitive way to obtain small models.

- michel2019heads: SUPPORTED — head pruning by gate-gradient importance. Abstract: "a large percentage of attention heads can be removed at test time without significantly impacting performance." Sec. 4.1: "As a proxy score for head importance, we look at the expected sensitivity of the model to the mask variables xi_h defined in § 2.3: I_h = E_{x~X} |dL(x)/d xi_h|" (also in earlier notes).
- molchanov2019taylor: SUPPORTED — Sec. 1: "we approximate it with a Taylor expansion (akin to [27]), resulting in a criterion computed from parameter gradients readily available during standard training." Sec. 3: "I^(1)_m(z) = (dE/dz_m)^2" at a gate (quoted in full under [14]).
- zhu2017prune: SUPPORTED — here the citation sits on "a gradual schedule", which is exactly their contribution. Sec. 3: "We introduce a new automated gradual pruning algorithm in which the sparsity is increased from an initial sparsity value s_i (usually 0) to a final sparsity value s_f over a span of n pruning steps" (cubic form quoted under [14]).
- xia2022cofi: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED — "the unit set of CoFi" is not the draft's set. CoFi: heads, FFN intermediate dimensions, entire MHA and FFN sub-layers (dropped "separately, instead of pruning them as a whole"), and a hidden dimension mask "shared across layers" (Sec. 3, quoted under [14]). The draft prunes heads, MLP channels and whole transformer blocks, and not hidden dimensions. Proposed wording: "We prune heads \citep{michel2019heads} and MLP channels with Taylor importance \citep{molchanov2019taylor} on a gradual schedule \citep{zhu2017prune}; these are the fine-grained units of CoFi \citep{xia2022cofi}, which additionally prunes whole attention or MLP sub-layers and a shared hidden dimension."
- xia2023sheared: SUPPORTED — Abstract: "we study structured pruning as an effective means to develop smaller LLMs from pre-trained, larger models." ... "Sheared-LLaMA models outperform state-of-the-art open-source models of equivalent sizes, such as Pythia, INCITE, OpenLLaMA and the concurrent TinyLlama models, on a wide range of downstream and instruction tuning evaluations, while requiring only 3% of compute compared to training such models from scratch." Sec. 2.1: "continue pre-training the pruned model with the language modeling objective. We refer to this second stage as continued pre-training." (abstract quotes also in earlier notes)
