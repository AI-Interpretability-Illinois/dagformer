\end{abstract} \section{Introduction} A transformer \citep{vaswani2017} composes its layers through residual connections \citep{he2016resnet}: every attention head and MLP reads the same running sum and writes back into it.

[2] keys=['elhage2021']
Mechanistic analyses describe this residual stream as the communication channel between components \citep{elhage2021}, and its capacity is shared by every layer, so the cross-layer connectivity of a transformer is both fixed and crowded.

[3] keys=['huang2017densenet', 'mhc2026', 'muddformer2025', 'pagliardini2024denseformer', 'zhu2024hyperconnections']
A line of work relaxes this by letting a layer read earlier layers' outputs directly: dense connections \citep{huang2017densenet}, depth-weighted averaging with learned static weights \citep{pagliardini2024denseformer}, and, most recently, hyper-connections \citep{zhu2024hyperconnections,mhc2026}, which make the mixing weights input-dependent, and multiway dynamic dense connections \citep{muddformer2025}, which also make them separate for the query, key, value and residual inputs of a block.

[4] keys=['muddformer2025']
These models reach the loss of transformers trained with substantially more compute \citep{muddformer2025}.

The backbone is an unchanged OLMo-2 \citep{olmo2} whose per-layer input is replaced by these mixtures; at initialization the weights select only the previous layer, so \dagf{} starts as exactly the dense model and learns to deviate from it.

[17] keys=['bapna2018transparent', 'menghani2024laurel', 'pagliardini2024denseformer', 'peters2018elmo']
In NLP, transparent attention \citep{bapna2018transparent} and ELMo's scalar mixture \citep{peters2018elmo} learn static weights over layer outputs, as does DenseFormer's depth-weighted averaging for decoder-only transformers \citep{pagliardini2024denseformer} and LAuReL \citep{menghani2024laurel}.

[18] keys=['mhc2026', 'zhu2024hyperconnections']
Hyper-connections \citep{zhu2024hyperconnections} widen the residual stream into several streams with learnable, optionally input-dependent, depth and width connections; mHC \citep{mhc2026} shows that the product of unconstrained mixing matrices across depth lets forward and backward signals explode or vanish, and projects the residual-mixing matrix onto the doubly stochastic manifold with Sinkhorn-Knopp to stabilize training at scale; in both, the coefficients of a layer are a function of that layer's incoming hidden state.

[19] keys=['muddformer2025']
MUDDFormer \citep{muddformer2025} generates per-position dense connection weights for the query, key, value and residual streams from the hidden state at each layer and reports matching transformers trained with 1.8 to 2.4$\times$ the compute.

[20] keys=['fedus2022switch', 'raposo2024mod', 'shazeer2017moe']
\paragraph{Per-token routing.} Mixture-of-experts layers route tokens to experts with a per-layer gate on the current representation \citep{shazeer2017moe,fedus2022switch}, and Mixture-of-Depths routes tokens around blocks the same way \citep{raposo2024mod}.

[21] keys=['michel2019heads', 'molchanov2019taylor', 'xia2022cofi', 'xia2023sheared', 'zhu2017prune']
\paragraph{Structured pruning.} We prune heads \citep{michel2019heads} and MLP channels with Taylor importance \citep{molchanov2019taylor} on a gradual schedule \citep{zhu2017prune}, the unit set of CoFi \citep{xia2022cofi}; Sheared LLaMA \citep{xia2023sheared} shows that structured pruning plus continued training is a competitive way to obtain small models.
